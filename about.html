About Fretboardwalk

Fretboardwalk is a free, independent tool for one specific job: helping guitarists actually know the fretboard — where every note and scale degree lives, in any key.

The problem it's built for

Most guitarists hit the same wall. You can play chords, maybe a scale shape or two, and get through songs — but ask you to point to a specific note anywhere on the neck, or explain how it relates to the key you're in, and it's a blank. The fretboard stays a maze of memorized shapes rather than a map you understand.

That gap is what Fretboardwalk exists to close.

The idea behind it

The core belief is simple: the fastest way to understand the guitar is to stop thinking in letter names and start thinking in scale degrees — the numbers 1–7 that stay the same in every key. Learn those, and the neck stops being twelve separate keys to memorize and becomes one pattern you can move anywhere.

So instead of another chart to stare at, Fretboardwalk turns it into a quick, game-like drill: you're shown a target degree, you find it on the neck, and you hear the real note played back. That tight loop of number → sound → position is what turns knowledge into instinct. You can start with the answers shown and hide them as you improve.

What it is (and isn't)

  • Free. No account, no sign-up, no ads. It runs entirely in your browser.
  • Independent. It's a small, self-funded project, not a company or a course trying to upsell you.
  • Focused. It does one thing — fretboard and scale-degree recall — and tries to do it well, rather than being an everything-app.
  • For real playing. The goal isn't to pass a theory quiz; it's to make you faster and freer on the actual instrument.

Learn the concepts

Alongside the trainer, there's a small, plain-English library aimed at beginners with no theory background:

  • The full guide — how it works and why the major scale matters.
  • How to memorize the fretboard — the practical method, with a full notes chart.
  • Scale degrees explained — the numbers that make the fretboard make sense.
  • The pentatonic scale — the five notes most solos are built on.
  • The minor scale — how "sad" is just major with three notes lowered.
  • The major/minor degree map — one shape, two keys, and the cycle of fourths.
  • Building chords from degrees — turn one barre shape into twelve chords.

Spotted a mistake or have a suggestion? Feedback is always welcome — it's how the tool gets better.
